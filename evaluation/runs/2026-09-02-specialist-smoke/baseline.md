## EVAL-013-split-pan-sauce

Your pan sauce probably broke because the butter got too hot or there was not enough liquid left to hold it together.

Take the pan off the heat. Whisk in a splash of warm stock, water, or lemon juice, then whisk until it looks smooth again. Add any remaining butter slowly and keep the sauce warm, not boiling. If it is very greasy, you may need to start with a few spoonfuls of liquid in a clean pan and whisk the broken sauce back in gradually.

Taste at the end because extra liquid or lemon can change the seasoning. The sauce is ready when it looks glossy and lightly coats a spoon.

## EVAL-014-dense-banana-bread

Dense, gummy banana bread usually comes from too much banana or liquid, overmixing, weak baking soda or baking powder, or underbaking.

Next time, measure the mashed banana instead of adding all the bananas you have. Mix the wet and dry ingredients only until combined. Check that your leavening is fresh. If the top browns before the center is done, lower the oven slightly or tent the loaf with foil and keep baking.

Use a tester in the center. It should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter. Let the loaf cool before slicing so the crumb can finish setting.

## EVAL-015-dry-chicken-breast

Chicken breast dries out easily because it is lean and often uneven in thickness. Pound or butterfly it to an even thickness, salt it ahead of time, and cook over medium heat instead of blasting it.

Use a thermometer to check that it is safely cooked; do not rely only on color or juices. Rest it briefly before slicing so the juices settle. If the chicken is already dry but safely cooked, slice it thinly and serve it with sauce, broth, dressing, or in a dish like salad, tacos, or soup.
