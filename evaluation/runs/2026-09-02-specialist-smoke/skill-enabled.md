## EVAL-013-split-pan-sauce

Observed state: the pan sauce was glossy, then turned greasy after butter was whisked in. Target state: a glossy sauce that lightly coats a spoon with no ring of free fat.

Likely mechanism: the butter emulsion broke. Common causes are too much heat, too little water phase after reduction, or adding fat faster than the sauce can hold it.

Recovery:

1. Pull the pan off the heat.
2. Add 1-2 teaspoons warm water, stock, wine, or lemon juice to rebuild the water phase.
3. Whisk hard until the sauce looks glossy again.
4. If it is still oily, start in a clean warm pan with 1 tablespoon liquid and whisk in the broken sauce a spoonful at a time.
5. Finish any remaining cold butter in small pieces off heat or over very low heat.

Side effects: water or stock dilutes seasoning; lemon or wine also changes acidity. Correct salt and acid only after the sauce is stable. Stop when it coats the spoon, looks shiny, and no oil pools at the edge.

## EVAL-014-dense-banana-bread

Observed state: dense, gummy center. Target state: tender quick bread with an open crumb and a fully set center.

Likely mechanisms to test in order:

1. Excess moisture: too much mashed banana or liquid relative to flour can prevent the center from setting.
2. Underbaking or pan load: a deep loaf can brown outside before starches and egg set in the center.
3. Weak leavening: old baking soda or baking powder gives poor lift.
4. Overmixing: too much gluten development makes the crumb tight and heavy.

Next batch, measure banana by weight or volume, keep the pan no more than about two-thirds full, and verify the leavener is fresh. Mix just until the last dry streaks disappear. If the loaf browns early, tent with foil and continue baking until the center is set rather than pulling it by color alone.

Verification cues: the top springs back near the center, a tester comes out with moist crumbs instead of wet batter, the loaf does not wobble in the middle, and the cooled slice shows small open holes instead of a compact wet line.

## EVAL-015-dry-chicken-breast

Safety gate: this is poultry, so `food-safety` stays active. Juiciness cannot override a verified safe endpoint. Current FoodSafety.gov guidance for chicken and poultry is 165 F (74 C) by food thermometer, reviewed 2024-11-21.

Observed state: dry, fibrous chicken breast. Target state: safely cooked chicken that stays as juicy as possible after rest.

Likely mechanism: chicken breast is lean, so excess heat, uneven thickness, and carryover can push the proteins past the point where they hold moisture well.

Next time:

1. Pound or butterfly breasts to even thickness.
2. Salt ahead or use a light brine if the recipe allows it.
3. Cook with gentler heat and use a thermometer in the thickest part.
4. Rest briefly before slicing.
5. Slice across the grain and serve with pan sauce, salsa, broth, yogurt sauce, or dressing.

For chicken already cooked safely but dry, do not try to solve the next batch by undercooking. Slice it thinly, add moisture at service, or repurpose it into soup, tacos, chicken salad, or sauced rice bowls. Do not rely on color or clear juices as the only safety test.
