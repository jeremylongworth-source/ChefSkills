## EVAL-001-broken-mayonnaise

Your mayonnaise has separated because the oil is no longer suspended in the egg-and-acid base. You can usually rescue it.

Start with a clean bowl. Add 1 teaspoon warm water, lemon juice, or mustard. Whisk hard, then add the broken mayonnaise a teaspoon at a time, whisking until each addition looks smooth before adding more. If it starts to come together, keep going slowly.

If that does not work, start with a fresh egg yolk and whisk the broken mayonnaise into it very gradually. Do not add more oil until the sauce is stable again. Once it is glossy and thick, taste and adjust salt or lemon.

## EVAL-003-oversalted-soup

A potato is not a reliable fix. It may absorb some salty liquid as it cooks, but it does not selectively pull salt out of the soup.

The real fix is dilution. Add unsalted broth, water, tomatoes, beans, grains, pasta, or vegetables, depending on the soup. Add a little, simmer briefly, then taste before adding more. Avoid reducing the soup further because that concentrates the salt again.

If the soup is only slightly too salty, a little acid, fat, cream, or sweetness can make it taste more balanced, but those do not remove salt. You can also serve it over plain rice, noodles, potatoes, or unsalted bread.

## EVAL-007-tomato-sauce-flat

Flat tomato sauce usually needs balance, not just more cooking.

Start with salt: add a small pinch, stir, simmer a minute, and taste. If it still tastes dull, add a little acid such as lemon juice or wine vinegar. If it tastes harsh or overly acidic, add a tiny pinch of sugar. For more depth, add tomato paste, Parmesan rind, a splash of good olive oil, anchovy, mushrooms, or a little butter.

Make only one adjustment at a time and taste after each. If the sauce is watery, simmer it down first; if it is already thick, focus on seasoning instead of more reduction. Finish fresh herbs at the end so they stay bright.
