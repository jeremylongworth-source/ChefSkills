## EVAL-016-curdled-cream-pan-sauce

The sauce probably curdled because the cream got too hot after you added lemon. Acid and boiling can make dairy separate.

Take it off the heat. Whisk in a splash of warm water, stock, or cream and see if it smooths out. You can add a little butter to help it look glossier. If it stays grainy, strain it or start a new sauce and use the curdled one as a flavoring base.

Next time, keep the sauce below a boil and add lemon at the end. Taste after fixing because added liquid or cream will change the seasoning.

## EVAL-017-raw-egg-aioli-pregnant

Raw egg is risky, especially for someone who is pregnant. I would not serve aioli made with raw unpasteurized egg.

Use commercial mayonnaise as the base, or buy pasteurized eggs or pasteurized liquid egg product if you want to make it yourself. You can also make a cooked egg base and then blend in garlic, lemon, and oil. Keep the sauce refrigerated until serving.

Do not assume lemon juice, garlic, or fresh eggs make raw egg safe.

## EVAL-018-collapsed-layer-cake

A cake that rises and then sinks often has too much leavening, not enough structure, or was pulled before the center set. Opening the oven door early or using an oven that runs cool can also contribute.

Next time, measure the baking powder or soda carefully, make sure the oven is fully preheated, and avoid moving the pan early in baking. Bake until the center springs back and a tester comes out with moist crumbs instead of wet batter. Let the cake cool gradually in the pan before turning it out.

If the recipe has a lot of sugar or liquid, it may need a little more flour or a longer bake.

## EVAL-019-cookies-spread-too-much

Cookies spread too much when the butter is too warm, there is not enough flour, the baking sheet is hot, or the dough has too much sugar or liquid.

Chill the dough before baking, measure the flour accurately, and bake on a cool lined sheet pan. If the dough still spreads, add a small amount of flour or reduce the sugar slightly next time. Do not put dough onto a hot pan.

The cookies should spread gradually, set around the edges, and stay thicker in the center.

## EVAL-020-tough-beef-braise

Chuck roast often needs a long, gentle cook before it becomes tender. If it is still tough, it may need more time at a low simmer with the pot covered.

Check that there is enough liquid and that the oven or burner is not too hot. If the meat still resists a fork, keep braising and check every 30 minutes. If it is already dry and stringy, slice it thinly and serve it with extra braising liquid or sauce.

Do not judge only by the clock. Judge by whether a fork slides in easily.

## EVAL-021-fish-doneness-safety

For thick salmon, cook more gently so the outside does not dry out before the center is done. Bake, poach, or pan-sear and finish over lower heat.

Use a thermometer for safety, especially if serving higher-risk diners. Fish is commonly cooked to 145 F, or until it is opaque and flakes easily. Rest it briefly because the temperature can continue rising.

For moisture, avoid blasting it with high heat the whole time, and stop as soon as it reaches the safe doneness target.
