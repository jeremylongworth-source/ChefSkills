## EVAL-002-thin-gravy

Don’t sprinkle dry flour straight into hot gravy. It will clump and can leave a raw, pasty taste.

First decide what the gravy needs: if it tastes watery, simmer it uncovered to reduce and concentrate flavor. Stir often and stop when it lightly coats the back of a spoon.

If the flavor is already good but the texture is too thin, thicken in stages. Whisk 1 tablespoon flour with 2 tablespoons cool water or stock until smooth, then whisk in a little at a time. Simmer 3 to 5 minutes after each addition so the starch fully thickens and the flour taste cooks out. A cooked roux or beurre manie also works and gives a rounder texture.

Stop when the gravy nappes the spoon. Too much flour makes it dull, heavy, and gluey, so add less than you think and give it time to show its final thickness.

## EVAL-006-chicken-counter-overnight

No. Discard the chicken.

Cooked chicken left on the counter overnight has spent too long at room temperature. Reheating may kill some bacteria, but it does not reliably make time-abused food safe because some hazards can leave toxins behind, and you cannot judge that by smell, taste, or appearance.

Do not taste-test it. Bag it, throw it out, and wash any container, utensils, or surfaces it touched.

For next time, refrigerate cooked chicken promptly in shallow containers so it chills quickly. Reheat only chicken that was stored safely.

## EVAL-005-sauce-for-forty

The scale factor is 10, but don’t blindly multiply every part of a pan sauce and expect it to behave the same.

Start by multiplying the main liquid and base ingredients by 10, then adjust the process. A sauce for 40 needs more surface area, not just a deeper pot, because reduction depends on evaporation. Use a wide rondeau, several pans, or reduce in batches, then combine.

Be conservative with salt, strong acids, spices, alcohol, and thickeners. Start around 7 to 8 times those, taste after reduction, then finish in stages. Butter, herbs, lemon, and delicate aromatics are usually best added near service so they stay fresh and emulsified.

Reduce to the same cue as the original: glossy, balanced, and lightly coating the spoon. Hold gently warm, not boiling, and whisk before serving. If it tightens too much, loosen with a splash of unsalted stock or water; if it is thin, reduce more before adding extra thickener.
