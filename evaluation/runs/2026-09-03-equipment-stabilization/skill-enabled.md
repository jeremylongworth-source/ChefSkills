## EVAL-036-dark-pan-cookie-browning

Decision: do not treat oven temperature as the only variable. The dark nonstick pan is probably driving the bottom browning.

Current state: cookie bottoms are nearly burnt while centers lag. Target state: set centers with controlled bottom color. Mechanism: dark pans absorb radiant heat and transfer it aggressively into the dough, so the base browns before the center structure sets.

Adjustment path:

1. Use a lighter-colored sheet pan if available.
2. Line the dark pan with parchment or a silicone mat if appropriate for the oven temperature.
3. Move the pan up one rack if bottom heat is strong.
4. Cool the pan between batches.
5. Chill warm dough if spread is contributing.
6. Lower temperature by 25 F only if pan and rack changes are not enough.

Verification cue: bottoms are golden rather than dark, edges are set, centers look puffed and no longer wet, and the cookies finish top and bottom at the same time.

## EVAL-037-glass-broiler-lasagna

Safety gate: do not put ordinary glass bakeware under the broiler unless the current manufacturer instructions explicitly say that exact vessel is broiler-safe.

Current state: hot lasagna in a glass dish with a pale top. Target state: browned cheese without glass breakage risk. Mechanism: a broiler applies intense direct radiant heat, which can create thermal stress in glass cookware that is not rated for direct heat.

Safer paths:

1. Transfer individual portions to a broiler-safe metal, cast iron, or otherwise approved dish.
2. Brown only the topping separately on broiler-safe cookware and add it back.
3. Use a torch if appropriate and safe for the topping.
4. Return the glass dish only to a conventional oven method allowed by its use instructions.

Verification cue: the browning step happens in cookware rated for the heat source; the quality goal does not override the physical hazard.

## EVAL-038-crowded-air-fryer-wings

Safety gate: do not stack raw wings as the plan. Shaking helps exposure, but it does not make a crowded air-fryer basket reliably cook raw poultry evenly.

Current state: raw chicken wings for a party, air fryer basket, proposed stacking. Target state: crisp wings with verified safe doneness. Mechanism: air fryers rely on hot airflow across exposed surfaces. Stacking blocks airflow, delays rendering and browning, and can leave underheated spots even if outer pieces look crisp.

Workflow:

1. Cook in a single layer or small batches.
2. Turn or shake pieces during cooking so all sides get airflow.
3. Keep raw-chicken utensils, plates, and marinades separate from finished wings.
4. Hold finished batches safely while the next batch cooks.
5. Check the thickest wings with a food thermometer and use the current safe poultry endpoint.

Verification cue: wings are crisp and browned, but safety is decided by internal temperature in the thickest pieces, not color, crispness, or clear juices.

## EVAL-039-induction-thin-pan-scorching

Decision: fix the equipment interaction before changing the cream-sauce formula.

Current state: thin stainless pan on induction, scorching in one spot on medium. Target state: smooth sauce without browned dairy solids. Mechanism: induction responds quickly and heats the magnetic zone directly. A thin pan can concentrate heat instead of spreading it, which scorches dairy before the sauce as a whole looks too hot.

Adjustment path:

1. Use a heavier induction-compatible pan if available.
2. Match pan diameter to the active burner zone.
3. Use lower power and let the sauce heat more gradually.
4. Stir continuously, especially across the hot spot.
5. Pulse heat off and on if the burner cycles aggressively.
6. If the sauce smells or tastes burnt, rebuild rather than trying to hide scorched dairy.

Verification cue: the pan bottom stays clean, the sauce smells sweet and dairy-rich rather than toasted or bitter, and the texture stays smooth without graininess.

## EVAL-040-high-altitude-pressure-cooker-beans

Decision: add time in controlled steps, but first check altitude-related variables and pressure-cooker limits.

Current state: beans remain firm around 7,000 feet in an electric pressure cooker. Target state: tender beans without vent blockage or dry cooking. Mechanism: altitude can change cooking behavior, and bean tenderness also depends on age, soaking, salt or acid timing, liquid ratio, pressure level, release method, and foaming.

Adjustment path:

1. Use fresh-enough beans and sort/rinse them.
2. Soak if the recipe supports it, especially for stubborn older beans.
3. Keep acidic ingredients out until the beans soften.
4. Use enough liquid for the cooker and bean volume.
5. Respect the pressure cooker's maximum fill line for foaming or expanding foods.
6. Adjust cook time or natural release in small increments instead of repeatedly adding large high-pressure cycles.

Verification cue: beans mash easily between fingers, skins are tender, liquid remains in the pot, foam has not blocked vents, and the cooker has stayed within manufacturer fill limits.

## EVAL-041-grill-flare-up-chicken-thighs

Safety gate: charred skin and clear juices are not enough to prove bone-in chicken thighs are safe.

Current state: fat flare-ups char the outside before the meat near the bone is done. Target state: browned chicken with verified safe poultry doneness. Mechanism: dripping fat ignites over direct heat while bone-in thighs heat slowly near the bone, so exterior color gets ahead of internal temperature.

Workflow:

1. Set up two-zone heat: direct side for browning, indirect side for finishing.
2. Start or move thighs to indirect heat when flare-ups begin.
3. Keep the lid and vents managed so the grill behaves like an oven during the finish.
4. Move pieces away from flames rather than spraying water onto the grill.
5. Use a thermometer in the thickest meat near the bone and follow the current safe poultry endpoint.

Verification cue: skin is browned rather than sooty, flare-ups are controlled, and the thickest meat near the bone reaches the safe internal temperature by thermometer.
