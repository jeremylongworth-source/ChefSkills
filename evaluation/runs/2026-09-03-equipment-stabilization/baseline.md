## EVAL-037-glass-broiler-lasagna

You can brown the top under the broiler for a short time, but watch it carefully because cheese can burn quickly. Keep the rack farther from the heating element.

If you are worried about the glass dish, transfer a serving to another pan or sprinkle extra cheese and bake it longer at a higher temperature.

## EVAL-038-crowded-air-fryer-wings

You can stack the wings if you shake the basket a few times, but they will crisp better in a single layer. For a party, work in batches if you have time.

Cook until they look browned and crisp, then check a thick wing with a thermometer if you have one. Wash anything that touched raw chicken.

## EVAL-039-induction-thin-pan-scorching

The recipe may be too delicate for medium heat. Lower the heat and stir more often. You can add a little more cream or liquid to help prevent scorching.

If the sauce tastes burnt, it may not be worth saving. For the next batch, start lower and do not leave the sauce sitting in one spot.

## EVAL-040-high-altitude-pressure-cooker-beans

At high altitude, beans can need more cooking time. Add a few more minutes of high pressure and check them again. Older beans may also take longer.

Soaking can help, and make sure there is enough water in the pot. Avoid adding acidic ingredients until the beans are tender.

## EVAL-041-grill-flare-up-chicken-thighs

Move the chicken away from flames and lower the grill heat. You can start the thighs skin-side down to render some fat, then flip and keep cooking until done.

If the outside is burning, finish them over a cooler part of the grill. Check that the juices run clear, or use a thermometer if you have one.
