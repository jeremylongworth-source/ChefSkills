## EVAL-008-evaluate-ai-sushi-recipe

Safety gate: do not treat ordinary grocery-store salmon as automatically safe for raw sushi.

The key issue is safety, not just freshness or quality. Good color, mild smell, or a "fresh" label cannot prove raw-fish safety. Raw salmon can carry parasites and harmful germs; freezing can address parasites in some fish, but it does not make raw seafood risk-free. Cooking seafood is the safest choice.

If the recipe keeps the salmon raw, revise it to require fish specifically intended for raw consumption from a reputable supplier, with clear handling history and prior freezing where appropriate. Keep it at 40 F or below, prepare it the day it is purchased or thawed, prevent cross-contact with ready-to-eat rice and vegetables, and discard leftovers that sat warm.

Safer home alternatives are cooked salmon, cooked shrimp, crab, egg, cucumber, avocado, or other fully cooked fillings. Do not serve raw or undercooked seafood to pregnant diners, young children, older adults, or immunocompromised guests.

## EVAL-010-gluten-free-cake-adaptation

Goal: keep the chocolate cake moist and cohesive while replacing wheat flour.

Wheat flour supplies starch, water absorption, and gluten structure. A straight swap to a random gluten-free flour can turn sandy or crumbly because rice, starch, nut, and bean flours bind and hydrate differently.

Use a reliable 1:1 gluten-free baking blend by weight. If the blend has no binder, add about 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum per cup of flour, or use a tested blend that already includes it. For celiac or allergy needs, use certified gluten-free ingredients and avoid shared contaminated tools.

Adjust texture in stages: add 1 extra yolk or 1-2 tablespoons yogurt, sour cream, oil, or milk for moisture and binding. Rest the batter 20 minutes before baking so starches hydrate. Bake until the center springs back and a tester shows moist crumbs, not a dry skewer.

If the test cake crumbles, increase binder or moisture slightly next batch. If it turns gummy, reduce gum or liquid and bake a little longer.

## EVAL-011-garlic-oil-storage

Safety decision: no, do not store fresh garlic cloves covered in oil on the counter.

Fresh garlic is low-acid and contains moisture. Covering it with oil creates a low-oxygen environment where botulism risk becomes the central hazard. This is a safety issue, not a spoilage-quality issue: dangerous garlic oil may still look, smell, and taste normal.

If the jar has been sitting at room temperature beyond brief handling or serving time, discard it without tasting. Do not try to make it safe by smelling it, boiling it, or adding it to another dish.

For a home-prepared garlic oil, keep it refrigerated at 40 F or below, label the date, and use it within 4 days under current CDC guidance. Freeze small portions if you need longer storage. Shelf-stable garlic-in-oil should come from a commercial or process-validated acidified product.
