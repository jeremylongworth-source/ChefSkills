## EVAL-008-evaluate-ai-sushi-recipe

Do not use ordinary raw grocery-store salmon for home sushi unless it is specifically sold as sushi/sashimi-grade and handled for raw consumption. Regular salmon can carry parasites or bacteria, and "fresh" does not mean safe raw.

Safer options: use cooked salmon, smoked salmon, canned salmon, shrimp, crab, avocado, cucumber, or egg. If you still want raw fish, buy from a reputable fishmonger, ask whether it is intended for raw use, keep it very cold, use it the same day, and avoid serving it to pregnant people, young children, older adults, or immunocompromised guests.

Also keep raw fish separate from rice, vegetables, boards, and knives until assembly.

## EVAL-010-gluten-free-cake-adaptation

Use a good 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend that contains xanthan gum, or add about 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum per cup of flour if your blend lacks it. Replace wheat flour by weight if possible.

To avoid crumbliness, increase moisture slightly: add 1 extra egg yolk, 2 tablespoons sour cream or yogurt, or 2 tablespoons oil. Let the batter rest 15-20 minutes before baking so the flour hydrates. Do not overbake; pull the cake when a toothpick has a few moist crumbs, not when fully dry.

Chocolate cakes adapt well because cocoa, fat, and moisture help structure. Cool fully before slicing.

## EVAL-011-garlic-oil-storage

No. Do not store fresh garlic cloves covered in oil on the counter.

Garlic in oil creates a low-oxygen environment where botulism toxin can develop, and the oil may look and smell normal even if unsafe. Counter storage is especially risky.

For homemade garlic oil, keep it refrigerated and use it within a few days, or freeze it in small portions. Discard it if it has been left at room temperature for more than a short serving period. Shelf-stable garlic oils should only be commercially prepared and properly acidified or processed.
