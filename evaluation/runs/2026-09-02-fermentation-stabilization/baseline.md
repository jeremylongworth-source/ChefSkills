## EVAL-026-kimchi-too-salty

If the kimchi is at pH 4.4, it is acidic enough for ordinary refrigerated kimchi handling, but that does not mean it is shelf stable. Put it in the refrigerator now.

Leaving it out longer will make it more sour, not less salty. You can drain or rinse some before serving, mix it into fried rice, pancakes, stew, or noodles, or serve it with unsalted rice and vegetables. Next time, salt the cabbage for a shorter time and rinse more thoroughly before packing.

## EVAL-027-cloudy-carrot-brine

Cloudy brine and bubbling can be normal signs of vegetable fermentation, especially if the smell is pleasantly sour and the carrots are under brine. Do not throw it out just because it is cloudy.

Keep everything submerged, watch for mold, slime, rotten smells, or mushy texture, and refrigerate when the ferment tastes sour enough. If the smell turns unpleasant or anything grows on the surface, discard it.

## EVAL-028-sluggish-sourdough-starter

A starter that bubbles but does not double is usually weak rather than unsafe, especially if it smells tangy and there is no mold. Feed it more consistently and keep it somewhere warmer.

Try discarding down to a small amount, feeding with equal weights of flour and water, marking the jar, and waiting until it rises predictably. Use it for bread only when it doubles reliably; until then, use it in pancakes or crackers where lift matters less. Discard it if you see mold or strange colors.

## EVAL-029-cold-sauerkraut-stall

At 58 F, sauerkraut will ferment slowly. If it smells normal and stays under brine, it may not be ruined.

Move it to a warmer room, keep it weighted down, and keep checking smell, texture, and bubbling. Do not heat it directly. Ferment longer if needed, then refrigerate when it is sour enough. Discard if you see mold, slime, or a bad odor.

## EVAL-030-fermented-hot-sauce-storage

Do not assume blended fermented hot sauce is safe in the pantry just because it tastes good or has vinegar added. Pantry storage needs a tested recipe or a verified safe pH and processing method.

The safer default is to refrigerate it. If you want shelf-stable jars, use a tested hot sauce canning recipe and follow the processing directions. Do not rely on taste alone.
