## EVAL-031-crowded-sheet-pan-vegetables

Try increasing the oven temperature and roasting the vegetables longer. You can also toss them with a little more oil and spread them out if possible.

If they are already wet, drain off liquid from the pan and return them to the oven until they brown. Cut larger pieces smaller next time so they cook faster.

## EVAL-032-small-skillet-stir-fry

You can make stir-fry in a skillet. Heat the pan well, add oil, cook the protein or vegetables, then add sauce at the end.

Because the pan is small, it may take longer. Stir often and avoid adding too much sauce early. If things get watery, cook a little longer to evaporate the liquid.

## EVAL-033-slow-cooker-frozen-chicken

Frozen chicken can cook in a slow cooker if it is left long enough, but it is better to thaw it first. Use the low setting for a long workday or high if you need it faster.

Check that the chicken is cooked through and no longer pink. If you have a thermometer, use it to make sure the center is done before shredding or serving.

## EVAL-034-microwave-casserole-cold-spots

Microwave it longer and stir it if you can. Dense casseroles can have cold spots, so let it sit for a minute or two and check whether it is hot all the way through.

If the plate is very hot but the middle is cold, move the center food to the outside and heat again. Use a thermometer if you are concerned.

## EVAL-035-pressure-cooker-canning-beans

Beans need pressure canning rather than water-bath canning. An electric pressure cooker may not work the same way as a pressure canner, so check the appliance manual.

If the manual says it supports canning, follow its directions carefully. Otherwise, use a pressure canner recipe for beans.
