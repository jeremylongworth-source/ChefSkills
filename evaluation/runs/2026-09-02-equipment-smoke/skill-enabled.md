## EVAL-031-crowded-sheet-pan-vegetables

Decision: do not start by turning the oven hotter. Fix the pan load and airflow first.

Current state: vegetables are crowded on a full sheet pan, pale, and wet. Target state: evaporated surface moisture and browned edges. Mechanism: crowding traps steam and gives the vegetables too little hot pan surface and airflow, so they cook by steaming before browning can build.

Adjustment path:

1. Spread the vegetables over two pans or roast in batches.
2. Dry the cut surfaces, oil lightly, and leave visible gaps between pieces.
3. Preheat the oven and pan if the vegetable can tolerate the hot start.
4. Use a rack position with good circulation and rotate pans if the oven has hot spots.
5. Raise temperature only after surface moisture and spacing are under control.

Verification cue: the vegetables sizzle, steam clears from the pan, edges brown, and liquid does not pool around the pieces.

## EVAL-032-small-skillet-stir-fry

Decision: use the skillet, but cook for four in batches. A small skillet cannot behave like a loaded wok.

Current state: no wok, small skillet, four servings. Target state: browned, crisp-tender ingredients rather than steamed vegetables. Mechanism: too much food drops pan temperature, reduces surface contact, and releases moisture faster than the skillet can evaporate it.

Workflow:

1. Prep everything before heating the pan.
2. Cook protein first if used, then remove it.
3. Cook vegetables in small batches, starting with dense or slower-cooking pieces.
4. Let the skillet reheat between batches.
5. Return everything to the pan only briefly with the sauce at the end.

Verification cue: each batch sizzles hard, browns in spots, and does not leave a layer of liquid in the pan.

## EVAL-033-slow-cooker-frozen-chicken

Safety gate: do not put frozen chicken breasts into a slow cooker before work as the plan. Thaw the chicken safely first.

Current state: frozen poultry plus slow gradual heating. Target state: chicken cooked from a safe starting condition and verified by thermometer. Mechanism: slow cookers heat gently, and frozen chicken slows the center's temperature rise, which can leave the food in the danger zone too long before it cooks through.

Safer path:

1. Thaw chicken in the refrigerator before slow cooking.
2. Use a tested slow-cooker recipe with the correct fill level and enough liquid/steam for the appliance.
3. Keep the lid on during cooking because heat recovery is slow after lid lifting.
4. Check the thickest part with a food thermometer and use the current safe poultry endpoint.

Verification cue: the chicken starts thawed, reaches the safe internal temperature, and the plan does not depend on unattended all-day cooking from frozen.

## EVAL-034-microwave-casserole-cold-spots

Safety gate: a hot plate is not proof that leftover casserole is safely reheated. Cold bites mean the dish needs a better reheating process.

Current state: leftover casserole reheated unevenly in a microwave. Target state: hot throughout with a verified safe internal temperature. Mechanism: microwaves heat unevenly, especially in dense or layered foods, so cold spots can remain until heat is redistributed by stirring, rotation, and standing time.

Reheat path:

1. Cover the casserole to hold steam.
2. Stir or rearrange the food so dense center portions move outward.
3. Rotate the dish if the microwave does not do it evenly.
4. Let it stand so heat can equalize.
5. Check multiple spots with a food thermometer and use the current leftovers reheating endpoint.

Verification cue: several internal spots reach the safe leftover temperature; the plate temperature does not decide safety.

## EVAL-035-pressure-cooker-canning-beans

Safety gate: do not pressure-can beans in an electric pressure cooker by copying pressure-canner timing.

Current state: low-acid beans plus an electric pressure cooker. Target state: preserved beans only through a tested pressure-canner process. Mechanism: canning safety depends on the full heat process, including venting, pressure stability, jar load, heat-up, and cool-down. Electric pressure cookers and small pressure cookers do not necessarily deliver the same process as a tested pressure canner.

Safe path:

1. Use an approved pressure canner and a tested bean canning recipe.
2. Do not convert the processing time to an electric pressure cooker or generic pressure cooker.
3. If you do not have the correct pressure canner, cook the beans for immediate eating, refrigerate, or freeze them instead.

Verification cue: the plan names a tested pressure-canner process for beans; appliance convenience does not substitute for preservation validation.
