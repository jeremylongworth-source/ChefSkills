## EVAL-022-low-salt-sauerkraut

I would not cut the salt in a sauerkraut recipe in half unless you are using a tested low-salt recipe. Salt affects more than flavor: it helps draw liquid from the cabbage, keeps the texture firmer, and supports the right fermentation.

For a lower-sodium result, use a smaller serving, rinse finished sauerkraut right before eating if the recipe allows, or make a refrigerated quick cabbage pickle instead of trying to ferment with much less salt. If this is for a medical sodium restriction, check with the person or clinician about what level is acceptable.

## EVAL-023-soft-fermented-pickles

Soft, slippery pickles with an unpleasant smell should not be rescued. Rinsing them will not make them safe.

Discard the batch. Next time, use a tested recipe, keep the cucumbers under brine, use the right salt level, start with fresh small cucumbers, and ferment at a moderate temperature. If you see mold, slime, or strong off odors, do not taste the pickles to decide whether they are safe.

## EVAL-024-kombucha-bottle-pressure

A bulging cap means the bottles have too much pressure. Move them cold if you can do so safely, handle them carefully, and do not keep letting them ferment warm in sealed bottles.

For the next batch, use less sugar or fruit for the second fermentation, shorten the bottle time, refrigerate promptly, and use bottles made for pressure. Check carbonation regularly. Kombucha keeps fermenting while sugar remains, especially when warm, so pressure can build.

## EVAL-025-hot-sauce-fermentation-plan

Use a tested fermented hot sauce recipe rather than guessing the salt or water ratio. Before starting, choose a clean glass jar, keep the peppers covered by brine, use a weight, leave room for gas, and ferment at a steady room temperature away from direct sun.

Check it daily for gas, brine coverage, mold, and smell. It should smell pleasantly sour, not rotten or putrid. Refrigerate the finished ferment or follow tested canning directions if you want shelf-stable storage. Do not assume a counter-fermented sauce is shelf-stable just because it tastes acidic.
